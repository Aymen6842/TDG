\chapter{Project Context and Problem Definition}

This chapter explains why the project exists and what problem it solves. It introduces the
host organization, describes the business context and the problem, studies the existing
alternatives, and presents the proposed solution with its expected outcomes.

\section{Introduction}

Organizations that cover several business activities --- software development, creative design,
marketing --- must coordinate their work across a growing number of tools and processes. As the
team grows, the problems grow with it: scattered project boards, manual attendance tracking,
disconnected calendars, informal notification channels, and no single view of what is happening
across the organization. This chapter presents that challenge in the specific context of Tawer
Digital Group, studies the available solutions, and introduces the platform built during this
internship to address it.

\section{Business Context}

This section looks at who the platform serves, what they need from it, and the limits it must
respect.

\subsection{Stakeholders}

The platform is an \textbf{internal tool} built exclusively for TDG employees. It is not
client-facing and is not exposed to external users. The primary stakeholders are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Executives} (CEO, CTO, CMO) --- organizational oversight, cross-unit visibility,
    and strategic decision-making.
  \item \textbf{Project managers and Scrum Masters} --- project lifecycle management, sprint
    governance, team coordination, and progress tracking.
  \item \textbf{Developers and designers} --- day-to-day task execution, time logging, and
    collaboration.
  \item \textbf{HR and administrative staff} --- employee onboarding, attendance monitoring, and
    team management.
  \item \textbf{DevOps engineers} --- infrastructure registration and uptime monitoring.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Users}

All TDG employees are users of the platform. The system defines approximately 31 role types to
model the different levels of responsibility across the two business units, from executives
down to individual contributors. Each user may hold one or more roles, and the platform
enforces what each role can see and do.

\subsection{Business needs}

TDG's daily operations involve several workflows. Together, they define the platform's scope:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Project management}: creating and tracking projects under either business unit,
    with team membership and email-based invitations.
  \item \textbf{Agile and flexible workflows}: supporting both structured agile delivery (epics,
    sprints, milestones, burndown and velocity analytics) and lighter free-form project
    management. The workflow type is chosen per project at creation time, regardless of
    business unit.
  \item \textbf{Task tracking}: a Kanban board with custom columns, WIP limits, dependencies,
    time logging, comments, and labels.
  \item \textbf{Attendance}: daily check-in and check-out (remote or on-site), with worked-hours
    computation and per-team statistics.
  \item \textbf{Calendar and events}: shared meetings and personal events with multi-channel
    reminders.
  \item \textbf{Notifications}: alerts delivered through in-app inbox, email, push notifications,
    Telegram, and ntfy.
  \item \textbf{Infrastructure monitoring}: registering company servers and services, with
    automated health checks and outage alerts.
  \item \textbf{AI-assisted insights}: a copilot that answers questions grounded in the
    organization's own project data, and an estimator that predicts task effort from historical
    outcomes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Constraints}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The platform must be \textbf{self-hosted and internal}, with no dependency on external
    project-management services for storing or exposing project data.
  \item It must support \textbf{bilingual use} (English and French) to accommodate the team's
    working languages.
  \item \textbf{Fine-grained access control} must govern every endpoint, since users from
    different roles and business units share the same system.
  \item The system must be \textbf{maintainable by a small team}, with a uniform codebase
    structure so that any module is easy to read once the pattern is learned.
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem Statement}

\textbf{What currently happens.} Before this project, TDG managed its work primarily through
\textbf{GitHub Projects} --- a lightweight project board integrated into the GitHub ecosystem.
Day-to-day coordination relied on informal channels: direct messages for reminders, manual
processes for attendance, and no centralized calendar, notification system, or infrastructure
monitoring.

\textbf{Why it is inefficient.} This setup has several limitations:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Fragmentation.} Project tracking, attendance, calendar, notifications, and
    infrastructure monitoring are handled by separate, disconnected tools --- or do not exist
    at all. There is no single place where a manager can see the state of a project, who is
    present today, and whether the company's servers are healthy.
  \item \textbf{Limited project modeling.} GitHub Projects provides flat task boards with no
    support for agile artifacts (epics, sprints, milestones, burndown charts), no distinction
    between agile and free-form workflows, and no per-project role-based access control
    matching TDG's organizational structure.
  \item \textbf{No attendance tracking.} Check-in and check-out, worked-hours computation, and
    presence statistics are handled outside any integrated tool, making compliance monitoring
    manual and error-prone.
  \item \textbf{No organizational intelligence.} Answering cross-cutting questions about project
    state --- ``what is blocking this sprint?'', ``how long did similar tasks take?'' --- requires
    manual searching across multiple sources, with no way to query the organization's own data
    in natural language.
  \item \textbf{Data exposure.} Hosting project data on a third-party platform means internal
    task descriptions, discussions, and workflow details are stored outside the organization's
    direct control.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Why improvement is necessary.} As TDG grows, these problems get worse. Each new team
member adds coordination overhead, and each new project spreads the work across more tools.
Without historical data, planning stays based on intuition rather than on evidence. The logical
next step is a unified, self-hosted platform that brings these workflows together and adds an
intelligent layer on top of the organization's own data.

\section{Existing Solutions Study}

Several established platforms cover parts of the problem described above. This section
examines three representative tools and compares them against TDG's specific needs.

\textbf{GitHub Projects.} GitHub Projects is a lightweight project-management feature built into
the GitHub ecosystem. It provides customizable board and table views linked to repository issues
and pull requests. Its main strength is its tight integration with the development workflow:
issues, branches, and pull requests connect directly to board cards at no additional cost.
However, it offers no native agile artifacts (epics, sprints with lifecycle governance,
milestone analytics), no attendance or time tracking, and no calendar or event management. It
also lacks multi-channel notifications, infrastructure monitoring, and AI-powered insights.
Project data is hosted on GitHub's infrastructure.

\textbf{Jira.} Jira~\cite{jira} is one of the most widely used project-management platforms. It
is known for its deep agile support: Scrum and Kanban boards, epics, sprints, and extensive
reporting. A large plugin ecosystem extends it into time tracking, test management, and CI/CD
integration. However, Jira provides no built-in attendance tracking, no integrated calendar or
company-wide event management, and no infrastructure monitoring. Its AI features (Atlassian
Intelligence) are cloud-only and rely on generic models, not on the organization's own project
content. The licensing cost is high for small teams, and the on-premise option (Data Center)
requires an enterprise license.

\textbf{ClickUp.} ClickUp is an all-in-one productivity platform that combines project
management, documents, goals, and time tracking in a single application. It offers tasks,
multiple views (list, board, Gantt, calendar), and built-in time tracking at a competitive
price. However, it has no attendance or check-in/check-out system, no infrastructure
monitoring, and no self-hosted deployment option. Its AI features are generic and not grounded
in the organization's project data. Notification channels are limited to in-app and email, and
its role-based access model is not fine-grained enough for a multi-unit organization with over
thirty role types.

No single existing tool covers the full scope of TDG's needs. Each platform addresses only
part of it: Jira is strong at agile project management, and ClickUp adds time tracking and
basic calendar features. None of them combines project management, attendance, calendar,
infrastructure monitoring, multi-channel notifications, and a data-grounded AI copilot in a
single, self-hosted, bilingual application governed by fine-grained role-based access control.
This integration, rather than any single feature, is what motivates the proposed solution.

\section{Proposed Solution}

The proposed solution is \textbf{Tawer Management}: a full-stack web platform that brings
TDG's operational workflows into a single, self-hosted application and adds an AI assistant
that works on the organization's own data.

\subsection{Main objectives}

The platform has four objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Unify operational workflows.} Provide a single web application covering project
    management, agile backlog, task tracking, personal to-dos, attendance, calendar, reminders,
    multi-channel notifications, and infrastructure monitoring.
  \item \textbf{Govern access across the organization.} Implement a role-based access-control
    model expressive enough for approximately 31 role types across the two business units,
    ensuring that each user sees and manages only what their role permits.
  \item \textbf{Add an intelligent layer grounded in real data.} Build a Retrieval-Augmented
    Generation (RAG) copilot~\cite{lewis2020rag} that answers questions strictly from the
    organization's own project content, with citations, and that estimates task effort from
    historical outcomes rather than from guesses.
  \item \textbf{Maintain a uniform, extensible codebase.} Enforce a strict, per-feature layered
    architecture so that every module follows the same structure, keeping the system
    maintainable as new domains are added.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{General workflow}

The platform is a \textbf{two-application system}: a stateless NestJS~\cite{nestjs} REST API
consumed by a separate Next.js~\cite{nextjs} web client, communicating over HTTP/JSON with
JWT-based~\cite{jwt} authentication. A user logs in and selects a project, or opens personal
features such as to-dos, attendance, or the calendar. Inside a project, the user manages tasks
on a Kanban board, tracks sprints, reviews analytics, or asks the AI copilot questions answered
from the project's own content. Notifications are delivered automatically through the user's
configured channels. Infrastructure monitoring runs in the background and alerts the
responsible managers when an outage is detected.

\subsection{Main features}

The platform covers ten operational domains, each built as an independent module following the
same layered architecture:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Authentication and access control} --- JWT-based login, token refresh, password
    reset, and a centralized RBAC catalogue applied uniformly across the API.
  \item \textbf{User and team administration} --- account provisioning, directory management, and
    team grouping.
  \item \textbf{Projects and membership} --- project lifecycle, business-unit scoping, team
    membership, and email-based invitations.
  \item \textbf{Agile backlog} --- epics, sprints with a governed lifecycle, milestones, and
    server-side burndown, velocity, and Gantt analytics.
  \item \textbf{Tasks and Kanban} --- a data-driven board with custom columns, WIP limits,
    dependencies, time logging, comments, and labels.
  \item \textbf{Personal to-dos} --- a private checklist with sub-tasks, priorities, and
    reminder-driven notifications.
  \item \textbf{Attendance} --- daily check-in and check-out (remote or on-site), worked-hours
    computation, and per-team statistics.
  \item \textbf{Calendar and events} --- shared meetings, personal events, and escalating
    multi-channel reminders.
  \item \textbf{Notifications and infrastructure monitoring} --- an in-app inbox, FCM push, email,
    Telegram, and ntfy delivery; automated ICMP and HTTP health checks with outage alerts.
  \item \textbf{AI copilot and estimation} --- permission-scoped RAG-based question answering with
    citations, and reference-class task-effort estimation from historical data.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Expected improvements}

Compared to the current GitHub Projects-based workflow, the platform is expected to remove
tool fragmentation and to automate manual processes (attendance, notifications, monitoring).
It gives the whole organization visibility through a single role-governed application, and it
adds an intelligent layer that turns the team's accumulated project data into concrete answers
and estimates.

\section{Expected Results}

Delivering the platform is expected to produce the following outcomes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A consolidated single source of truth.} One authenticated, bilingual application
    replacing the disconnected tools used across the agency's operational workflows.
  \item \textbf{Faster and more efficient workflows.} Removing the constant switching between
    tools, automating attendance recording, and providing real-time analytics all reduce the
    manual overhead of daily operations.
  \item \textbf{Improved user experience.} A unified interface tailored to TDG's structure ---
    two business units, agile and free-form project types, fine-grained roles --- provides each
    user with exactly the features and data relevant to their role.
  \item \textbf{AI-assisted decision support.} A copilot that answers questions from the
    organization's own content, with citations, replaces manual cross-project searches. An
    estimator that predicts effort from historical data supports evidence-based planning.
  \item \textbf{Automated monitoring and alerting.} Infrastructure health checks and multi-channel
    outage notifications reduce the time to detect and respond to incidents.
  \item \textbf{Governed, fine-grained access.} A centralized RBAC model lets approximately 31
    role types share one API safely, ensuring data isolation between business units and between
    projects.
  \item \textbf{A maintainable, extensible codebase.} A strict four-layer, per-feature
    architecture gives every module the same structure. Once the pattern is learned, any module
    is easy to read, which lowers the cost of onboarding and of adding new domains.
\end{itemize}

\section{Chapter Conclusion}

This chapter placed the project in its organizational and business context. Tawer Digital
Group, a hybrid digital agency, needed to replace a fragmented set of tools --- centered on
GitHub Projects --- with a unified, self-hosted platform adapted to its specific needs. The
study of existing solutions confirmed that no single tool on the market covers the full
required scope: project management, agile backlog, attendance, calendar, notifications,
infrastructure monitoring, and data-grounded AI assistance in one bilingual, role-governed
application. The proposed solution, Tawer Management, fills this gap. The next chapters explain
how it was built: Chapter~2 presents the methodology, architecture, and technology stack;
Chapters~3 and~4 describe the five development sprints that built the delivery platform and the
operational layer; and Chapter~5 covers the AI sprint, the project's main technical
contribution.
