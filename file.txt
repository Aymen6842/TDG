Chapter 1: Context & Problem Statement

This chapter frames the work: the organization and internship it was carried out in, the business context
of the product, the problem it addresses, the state of existing solutions, the proposed solution and its
objectives, and the outcomes expected of it. The remaining chapters then tell the build story sprint by
sprint (Chapters 2–3), with the AI sprint as the technical climax (Chapter 4).

1.1 Host organization & internship context

The host organization is **Tawer Digital Group (TDG)**, a digital agency; its full presentation is left to
the author to supply.




1.2 Business context

The system is the **internal operations platform of Tawer Digital Group (TDG)**, a digital agency. Its
scope and vocabulary are read directly from the domain model rather than assumed. Tawer is organized into
two business units, and this split is encoded directly in the schema rather than left to convention
: Tawer Dev runs software projects the `AGILE` way, tracked through epics, sprints, tasks, and
milestones, while Tawer Creative runs design and marketing projects `FREESTYLE`, without that structure.

Concretely, the model carries a `BusinessUnit` enum (`TawerDev`, `TawerCreative`) and a `ProjectType` enum
(`AGILE`, `FREESTYLE`), and the same split is visible in the seed dataset. Because the distinction is a
first-class part of the data model, it flows through the whole platform: a project belongs to a business
unit, and its type selects which workflow (agile backlog vs. free-form) the project follows.



The platform unifies, in a single application, every operational workflow the agency runs:
managing client **projects** and their **agile backlog** (epics → sprints → tasks, milestones), tracking
**personal to-dos**, recording **work sessions** (check-in/out attendance), scheduling **events/meetings**
on a calendar, sending **reminders** and **notifications** across channels (in-app, push, ntfy, Telegram,
email), and **monitoring servers and services**. The differentiator is an **AI copilot** that answers
questions grounded in the organization's own project content and estimates task effort from historical
data.

1.3 Problem statement (problématique)

A growing agency typically runs its work across many disconnected tools: one app for projects, another for
the agile backlog, spreadsheets for attendance, a separate calendar, ad-hoc channels for reminders and
notifications, and isolated dashboards for infrastructure health, with no single place to ask questions
about project state. This fragmentation forces constant context-switching and produces inconsistent data;
organization-wide questions ("what is blocking this sprint?", "how long will this task take?") become
effectively unanswerable without manual digging.

The platform's purpose is therefore to consolidate these workflows into one authenticated, localized (en/fr)
web application (serving roughly 31 distinct role types over a single REST API of ~146 endpoints), and to
add an intelligent layer on top: an AI copilot that answers questions grounded in the organization's own
content and estimates task effort from past work. Two design tensions frame the whole project:

1. Breadth vs. consistency: covering ten domains in one product risks a sprawling, inconsistent codebase.
   The response is a strict, uniform engineering structure so every module reads the same way (addressed in
   objective 4).
2. Intelligence vs. trust: an AI assistant is only useful if its answers are grounded and citable rather
   than hallucinated. The response is Retrieval-Augmented Generation over the platform's own data, with
   citations and an offline evaluation harness (addressed in objective 3, detailed in Chapter 4).

 1.4 Étude de l'existant (state of existing solutions)



The positioning argument the product itself supports (to be substantiated against the tools above) is that
mainstream tools each cover a slice: Jira and Linear for agile backlogs, Asana and ClickUp for general task
and project management. None of them bundle, for a single agency, agile and free-form projects, attendance,
calendar, infrastructure monitoring, multi-channel notifications, and a data-grounded AI copilot in one
localized, role-rich system. That integration, rather than any single feature, is the platform's thesis.

 1.5 Proposed solution & objectives

REST API consumed by a Next.js web client) that consolidates the agency's operational workflows and layers
a Retrieval-Augmented AI copilot on top. The high-level context is shown in Figure 1.1.

The solution pursues four objectives:

1. A single unifying web client covering projects and membership, an agile backlog (epics → sprints →
   tasks, milestones), personal to-dos, time and attendance (check-in/out), events and calendar, reminders,
   multi-channel notifications, and infrastructure monitoring.
2. A role-based access-control (RBAC) model expressive enough for roughly 31 role types across the two
   business units.
3. A production-grade RAG subsystem: outbox-based indexing, embedding generation, hybrid vector and lexical
   retrieval, an LLM reranker, grounded answering with citations, and size-aware effort estimation,
   validated by an offline evaluation harness.
4. A uniform, maintainable engineering structure so any of the roughly 20 backend modules and 10 frontend
   modules reads the same way.

Objectives 1–2 establish breadth and access control; objective 4 keeps that breadth maintainable; objective
3 is the differentiator and the technical climax of the report (Chapter 4).

 1.6 Expected outcomes

Delivering on the four objectives above is expected to produce four matching outcomes.

Objective 1 yields a consolidated single source of truth: one authenticated, bilingual (en/fr) application
that replaces a patchwork of disconnected tools across the agency's ten operational domains.

Objective 2 yields governed, fine-grained access: an RBAC model that lets roughly 31 role types share one
API safely across the Tawer Dev and Tawer Creative business units.

Objective 3 yields trustworthy AI assistance: a copilot whose answers are grounded in and cited to the
organization's own project content, plus effort estimates derived from historical data. Quality claims here
are backed by an offline evaluation harness rather than simply asserted (Chapter 4).

Objective 4 yields a maintainable, extensible codebase: a strict four-layer, per-feature convention
(controller → service → repository → DTO) that makes any of the roughly 20 backend and 10 frontend modules
readable once the pattern is learned. This convention is what keeps onboarding cost, and the cost of adding
an eleventh domain, low.

These outcomes are measured against the verified system totals used throughout this report: 146 endpoints
across 20 controllers, and 55 Prisma models and 25 enums across the schema, established by grepping the
source rather than estimated.
